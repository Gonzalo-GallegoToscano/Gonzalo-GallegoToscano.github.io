Calibrating an arbitrage-free SVI volatility surfaceIn progress
2026 · Python, optimisation, implied volatility, no-arbitrage constraints

Building an implied volatility surface from listed option chains using the raw SVI parameterisation, fitting one five-parameter slice per maturity to market implied variances by non-linear least squares.

The focus of the project is arbitrage: constraining each fitted slice so that the risk neutral density stays positive (no butterfly arbitrage) and so that total implied variance is non-decreasing in maturity (no calendar spread arbitrage), rather than simply fitting the quotes as closely as possible. Work in progress covers data cleaning of the chains, choice of initial values and parameter bounds, interpolation between maturities, and diagnostics comparing fitted against observed smiles.

Predicting NBA field goals with Bayesian logistic regression
2025/26 · Python, R, Bayesian inference, MCMC

Modelled whether an NBA shot is made using Bayesian logistic regression, on the 2014/15 SportVU shot log of 128,069 attempts and 21 variables. Python was used for cleaning and exploratory analysis, R for the modelling.

The posterior of the coefficient vector has no closed form, so estimation was implemented from scratch with a random walk Metropolis Hastings sampler: weakly informative priors, a tuned multivariate proposal, burn-in, and posterior summaries with credible intervals and trace plots. Covariates included shot distance, closest defender distance, shot clock, dribbles, shot type, location and shot number. Predictive accuracy was assessed by k-fold cross validation and benchmarked against a frequentist GLM, giving around 0.61 accuracy, with shot distance and defender proximity the dominant effects and a large irreducible random component in shot outcomes.

One week Value at Risk on the DAX and SPX
2025/26 · R, ARMA and GARCH, copulas, Monte Carlo

Estimated the one week 95% and 99% Value at Risk of an equally weighted, weekly rebalanced DAX and SPX portfolio, using weekly log returns from 2000 to 2025.

Both series showed negative skew, excess kurtosis of roughly seven and clear volatility clustering, so each margin was fitted with an ARMA–GARCH model with skewed Student-t innovations, selected on information criteria and validated with Ljung–Box tests on standardised residuals and their squares. The standardised innovations were mapped to uniforms by the probability integral transform, uniformity was checked with Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Anderson–Darling tests, and a bivariate copula was selected by AIC across elliptical and Archimedean families; a survival BB1 copula was retained for its asymmetric tail dependence. VaR was then obtained from 50,000 Monte Carlo scenarios, compared against Gaussian, Student-t, Clayton, Gumbel, Frank and historical benchmarks, and backtested over 1,355 weeks with Kupiec and Christoffersen tests, both consistent with nominal coverage and independent exceedances.

Modelling the 2016 EU Referendum
2024/25 · R, generalised linear models, clustering, PCA

Modelled the share of Leave votes across 1,070 UK wards using around 49 demographic, socioeconomic, procedural and geographic covariates drawn from the 2011 census, with 803 wards used for fitting and 267 held out for prediction.

Regions were grouped by hierarchical clustering, which separated London from the rest of the country and reduced nine regions to four cluster groups usable as a covariate and in interactions. Because ethnicity shares sum to one hundred and are strongly correlated, principal components replaced the raw variables, with the first two components explaining 85% of the variance and contrasting White against minority majority wards and Black against Pakistani populations. Linearity in the logit was tested using Box–Tidwell, age bands and social grades were regrouped, and interactions such as education with ethnicity and tenure with the White share were explored to reach a parsimonious final model, assessed through deviance residual plots and QQ plots.

Property price modelling
2024/25 · R, linear models, diagnostics

Built a normal linear model for the sale price of 500 properties, with covariates covering size (bedrooms, other rooms, total rooms, floor area), age, distance to the station, district and property type.

Model building was driven by exploratory analysis rather than a purely algorithmic search: collinear room counts were replaced by a single total, district was treated as a factor, multicollinearity was checked with variance inflation factors, and candidate interactions such as age with district and area with district were compared. The final model was assessed with residual, leverage and QQ diagnostics, and interpreted in terms of price per square metre by district, the discount on flats and terraced houses relative to detached properties, and the varying effect of age across districts.
